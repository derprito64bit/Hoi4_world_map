## Looks right (checked, no finding)
- **No kink or fold at λb** in any variant (`lambda_b_north.png`, `reg_malay.png`, `reg_random1.png`, `reg_eaustralia.png`, `reg_japan.png`). Coastlines and graticule meridians cross the λb meridians smoothly: Sumatra / Malay Peninsula and the Peru coast at λb 90 (100.9° E, −79.1°); Kyushu, Arnhem Land and New Guinea at λb 120 (130.9° E); Baja and the Rockies at −109.1°; Gujarat, the Amazon mouth and Greenland at λb 60. Meridians always bend toward the centre toward the pole, never outward, so there is no fold. This matches the C² selftest.
- **Seam continuity**: x is mirror-symmetric about the seam, so the east–west scale is the same on both sides and meridians are vertical right at the edge. Coastlines cross smoothly (Aleutians, Chukotka ↔ Alaska rows). Land within 50 px of the seam: 1221 / 1051 / 832.
- **The four curved off-globe edges are gone**: off-globe pixels are only the 28 margin rows (143 360 px), against 1.31 M in EE. No hard edges and no wedge.
- **Interior is true EE** (full-res mask, pixel-identical to EE): Europe, the Balkans, the Middle East and Africa 100 % in all three. India 99.7 % (λb 60) and 100 %. Caribbean 96.8 / 99.9 / 100 %. East China 87 / 94 / 99.99 %. At λb 90 India, Southeast Asia west of 101° E and all of South America are EE. At λb 120 all of China, Japan (1.08), SE Asia, the US east of the Rockies and western Australia are too.
- **60° S crop edge**: rows unchanged. South Georgia, the South Sandwich Is. and the Falklands are present (`reg_south60.png`, partly behind the caption, see #9).
- **Tiny islands**: land pieces < 9 px: 472 (EE) → 421 / 420 / 436. The band slightly enlarges Pacific islands. Hawaii 1.07–1.14, Fiji 1.05–1.10, Polynesia fine (`spacific_panorama.png`, `reg_polynesia.png`).
- **Random regions** (seed fcbefb7): random1, Peru coast, crosses λb 90 smoothly. Random2, Madagascar / Indian Ocean: 100 % identical. Random3, equatorial Pacific: 99.94 % identical (stretch ≈ 1 near the equator in every variant).

No variant shows a seam artefact, a kink at λb, or lost islands. The top-edge problem (#2) is the same in all three and needs its own decision (Q-014).

**Recommendation: λb = 90°.** It is the only option that keeps every heavily played region except the Pacific rim in true Equal Earth and still keeps the far north-east stretch around ×2. The places it widens (Japan, Korea, the US West) actually look more natural than in pure EE. λb 120 gains pure EE for China, Japan and the US but makes the Alaska–Chukotka–Kamchatka corner visibly smeared (×2.4–3, 6:1 local aspect), and that corner is on screen whenever the player pans across the wrap. λb 60 removes most of the Equal Earth character. If the owner wants China, Japan and the US to be exactly EE, λb 120 is defensible, but expect the north-east corner to look wrong. Either way, consider asking for a ramp-0.25 preview (#1) before locking the value. The decision is the owner's.
